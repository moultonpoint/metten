# Kalshi BTC 15-minute inputs: one external research pass

Research date: November 14, 2026. Prepared as the single research contribution to Connor’s Metten issue #5 brainstorm. This is an information shortlist, not an implementation plan or permission for trading.

## Recommendation for Connor

Keep BRTI as the primary reference, with Coinbase and Kraken explicitly identified as backup spot prices. That matches the contract’s reference and Connor’s preference; this research did not measure superior BRTI availability or uptime.

The best small additions are (1) retaining the settlement-average state that Kalshi already supplies, (2) retaining direction and size on Kalshi trades already planned, and (3) adding signed BTC spot trades from one existing backup provider. Changes in liquidity are worth examining using the planned Kalshi book stream before adding a separate spot order book. Scheduled announcement times are a small contextual addition. None has demonstrated incremental profit for Metten.

## Current settlement and delivery: verified distinctions

**Contract reference.** The anonymous [KXBTC15M series response](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series/KXBTC15M) linked [CRYPTO contract terms](https://assets.kalshi.com/contract_terms/CRYPTO.pdf). Those terms define a preceding-60-second CF index average, distinguish threshold comparisons, and exclude revisions made after expiration from the expiration value. The generic template contains placeholder timing; use the specific listing to instantiate it.

**Specific current listing.** Inspected [market KXBTC15M-26NOV141415-15](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets/KXBTC15M-26NOV141415-15), discovered through the open-market endpoint. Its primary rule compares the BRTI minute before 14:15 EST on November 14 with the BRTI minute before 14:00 EST; equality resolves YES. Secondary rules specify 60 RTI prices and rounding the final average to two decimals. Its opening threshold was $65,498.96 and `strike_type` was `greater_or_equal`. Keep listing rules and threshold authoritative; do not substitute the first observed tick. Kalshi’s [crypto help article](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823838-crypto-markets) independently describes opening-minute versus closing-minute averages and notes that market terms control.

**Three different live values.** The [per-second CF feed documentation](https://docs.kalshi.com/websockets/cfbenchmarks-value.md) states that ticks arrive roughly once a second. `avg_60s_data` uses the prior trailing window `[source_ts−60000, source_ts)`; its count excludes the current tick and its empty-history fallback is the current value. `last_60s_windowed_average_15min` exists only during quarter-hour final-minute accumulation, with window `(close−60000, close]`: start excluded, close included, counts progressing toward 60. An early partial mean is not a completed 60-sample settlement value. Documented feed boundaries and the listing’s “before close” wording require care; do not silently replace one convention with the other. Compare observed final fields to official settlement before relying on an exact reconstruction.

**Faster ticks are separate.** The [5Hz sibling feed](https://docs.kalshi.com/websockets/cfbenchmarks-value-5hz.md) offers up to five updates per second for BRTI and a limited group of other indices, requires authentication and carries raw parsed ticks without either averaging field. Its documentation says duplicate/out-of-order upstream timestamps are ignored. Do not average all 200ms observations and call that the contract’s 60 one-second observations. Subscription acknowledgment alone does not prove index availability.

**Methodology is not transport.** CF’s October 27 [first-party article](https://www.cfbenchmarks.com/blog/who-signs-the-price-the-cme-cf-real-time-indices-through-cfb-oracle) describes BRTI calculation/publication every 200ms since May 2026, referencing Methodology v17.0 sections 4–7. It describes a consolidated constituent order book, a size cap and stale/outlying venue screens. This is the administrator’s current description, not an independently repeated methodology audit. It does not establish Kalshi delivery latency or uptime. Its settlement example is hourly KXBTCD, not direct BTC15m evidence.

The [REST passthrough](https://docs.kalshi.com/cfbenchmarks/rest-passthrough.md) uses Kalshi authentication without a separate CF API key, but explicitly requires appropriate entitlement. History is documented, including some intra-second observations; it can contain later revisions. Passthrough calls also have a larger read-budget cost. Public evidence establishes available interfaces, not Connor’s entitlement or permitted redistribution/use. A public bot’s statement that BRTI requires its own paid license is therefore insufficient to describe all current Kalshi access routes.

**Derived implication, not a tested signal:** with a complete prescribed 60-sample window, known opening threshold K, n accumulated observations of mean A, and no rounding ambiguity, the remaining observations would need mean `(60K−nA)/(60−n)` for equality. This illustrates how much of settlement is already fixed. It fails as an exact calculation when samples, boundaries, rounding or authoritative observation selection differ. Never fill missing observations with future data or claim certainty from a partial mean.

## Timing, historical data and practical access limits

Historical candles summarize an interval; they cannot recover the trade order, signed flow, cancellations, queue state, or executable Kalshi quote at the moment of a decision. They can initialize trailing volatility and completed-bar calculations. A partially formed candle and a finalized historical candle are not interchangeable. The aaryann author reports Coinbase candle lag and uses a ticker for “now”; that lag report was not independently measured here.

Kalshi supports default immediate book deltas and optional 100/1000ms conflation. Netted updates can hide additions/removals that cancel within the batch; historical or conflated data cannot validate a sub-batch event-flow claim. Sequence gaps require a replacement snapshot or documented resubscription procedure. Per-subscription sequence is not a universal market clock. Coinbase trade batching and Kraken multi-trade messages likewise require per-trade timestamps and IDs, not one guessed order per message. Feed publication, server-send time and local receipt are separate clocks.

Keep source timestamp, receipt timestamp, channel/venue, sequence/gaps, reconnect/warmup and completeness alongside candidates. These are already planned source-quality concepts; retain their consequences rather than calling stale values a new signal. A feature must use what arrived by the decision time. Matching historical observations by source time alone can invent an unavailable lead. After a gap, suppress/reset dependent flow windows until coverage is adequate; do not bridge unknowable events.

Official public interfaces do not prove authorization for every geography, commercial use or redistribution. Kalshi authenticated streams and CF entitlement were not tested. Direct CF commercial licensing, provider terms, instrument availability, rate limits and geographic access need the lead’s later verification before an implementation decision. A public author’s Binance HTTP 451 report is one author’s experience, not a finding about Connor’s access. No legal conclusion is offered. No paid source is necessary to investigate the leading candidates on paper, subject to actual access and terms.

## Minimum fair evaluation, proposed only

1. Freeze a baseline using current reference distance, time remaining and realized volatility. Also compare against contemporaneous Kalshi odds so rediscovering the market’s existing forecast is not called an edge.
2. Add one candidate at a time with a frozen definition and horizon. Split chronologically by complete market/window and day; keep all snapshots from a window together. Reserve untouched final periods after exploratory choices. Include quiet, volatile, weekend and announcement periods without cherry-picking.
3. Use actual rules and official settlement labels. Never train on the completed final-minute average, later-revised history, completed future candle, or future fill when predicting earlier in that window. Fit normalization/calibration only on the training period. Do not transfer near-expiry findings to early-window decisions.
4. Compare out-of-sample log loss/Brier score and calibration, then incremental executable outcome at the ask/bid available after measured decision and delivery latency. Include current applicable fees/rounding, spread, available depth, slippage, misses and partial fills. For maker claims include queue uncertainty and adverse selection. A touched quote does not guarantee a fill. No fixed fee rate is endorsed from copied bot constants.
5. Report uncertainty by independent windows/days, not thousands of correlated ticks, and disclose all candidates tried. Reject small/inconsistent gains, gains confined to one chosen day, and gains that disappear at realistic costs. Better prediction can still have no trading value. A useful outcome may instead be identifying when to abstain or when execution is expensive.

No tests, historical replay, entitlement checks, latency measurements or profitability studies were run. Author screenshots and backtests are not reproduced or treated as proof. In particular, “high win rate” is uninformative without the price paid and losses. The strongest negative evidence in this pass is that public authors with several signals still report failing to improve on Kalshi’s price, and that some advertised features are only logged.

The lead should verify consequential citations before final owner delivery. This research stops here.
