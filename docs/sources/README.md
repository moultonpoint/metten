## What the named values mean

- **Kalshi contract values:** bids, buyable asks and last trades for an exact YES/NO contract. A trade is not an executable ask. Missing liquidity is unavailable, not a zero price. `selected_side` resolves to the concrete side considered by the engine; it is not a provider field.
- **BRTI:** the explicitly named index, delivered through Kalshi's selected per-second channel. Its raw tick, trailing average and partial final-minute average are different values. None automatically establishes the market's opening or settlement reference.
- **Coinbase and Kraken:** separate named BTC/USD last trades. Neither can restore missing BRTI or a missing Kalshi book. Their outage effects follow the strategy's actual dependencies.
- **Market references:** established, market-rule-defined opening and settlement values. A first tick, YES/NO result or contract payout cannot substitute for either.

The [learning strategy](../../examples/learning.json) declares Kalshi asks only. The other bindings are already supported by the rule language, but language support does not prove provider feasibility or access.

## Common acceptance boundary

These are E1 requirements, not provider guarantees. Preserve provider identity/time, monotonic local receipt time, subscription identity, connection/recovery generation and local accepted transaction order separately. Source UTC timestamps do not impose a cross-provider order. All same-millisecond genuine transactions retain accepted order.

Map initialization with a value to `Resynchronize`; metadata and acknowledgments carry no price observation. Only a genuine new identity can produce `Observation`. Equal-price new identities count; duplicates, replay, snapshots, heartbeats and timers do not. Apply coherent changes atomically before evaluation. Provider payloads stay outside the engine.

Mapped raw values must fit the existing exact decimal contract: at most twelve integer digits and six fractional places, contract cents in `[0,100]`, USD/BTC positive. Convert units exactly; reject incompatible input rather than silently round or pass through binary floating-point. Provider examples establish examples, not maximum precision guarantees. Quantity and direction remain source details, not added JSON inputs.

`Validity` is a substantiated current-value deadline. `CoverageFact` certifies a particular interval, separately from validity. No row below currently supplies sufficient proof for complete quiet intervals. Do not emit coverage from socket activity, an acknowledgment, a replacement snapshot or an assumed update cadence. Unknown freshness also prevents a value being labeled strategy-usable, even if a last received value can be displayed with its age and limitation.

The engine's completed millisecond cells cannot be filled retrospectively. A gap remains unavailable through every dependent lookback, including composed calculations. Collect only admitted post-Go history. Contract history stays with its exact contract; proven underlying history can span markets. Expiry and market boundaries precede eligibility; initial readiness exactly at open waits for the following full market.

### BRTI averages are details, not additional bindings

B1 documents `avg_60s_data` over `[source_time−60000,source_time)`, excluding the current tick and falling back to it when the count is zero. `last_60s_windowed_average_15min` is a partial final-minute accumulation with prose boundaries `(close−60000,close]`, count progressing to 60. Keep value, count and window together; a partial count is not a completed reference.

**U5:** the example's `window_end_ts_exclusive` label needs reconciliation with close-inclusive prose before reference use. Neither field proves the exact contract sample selection/rounding or equals the engine's millisecond time-weighted average. These details may retain provider precision as labeled details; do not feed them into JSON inputs or add an operator. No 5Hz or history route is introduced.
